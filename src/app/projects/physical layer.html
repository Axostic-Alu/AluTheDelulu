A scroll through Layer 1 of the OSI model: the cables, light and radio that carry every bit.

The Physical layer moves raw bits across a medium. It decides what a one and a zero look like as voltage, light or radio, how fast they move, and which plugs and cables carry them.

Where Layer 1 sits

Three ways to carry a bit

Copper

Twisted pair carries electrical signals. Twisting the two wires cancels most of the noise picked up from nearby cables.

  • Cat5e: 1 Gbps to 100 m
  • Cat6a: 10 Gbps to 100 m
  • Cat6: 10 Gbps to 55 m

Cheap and easy to install. The signal fades past 100 m and can pick up interference.

Fiber optic

Light pulses travel down a glass core. Multimode fiber suits buildings and campuses; single-mode fiber crosses cities.

  • Multimode: hundreds of meters
  • Single-mode: 10 km and beyond
  • 10 Gbps and up

Immune to electrical noise and very long reach. Costs more and needs careful splicing.

Wireless

Radio waves through open air, as in Wi-Fi. Everyone nearby shares the same airtime.

  • Tens of meters indoors
  • Shared medium
  • Walls weaken the signal

No cables to run. Slower and less stable than wire, and easier to eavesdrop on.

Encoding

Scroll to send the bits 10110010 down the wire. Manchester puts a change in the middle of every bit, so the receiver can keep time.

Clock and bit rate

A clock tells the receiver when to read each bit. The bit rate says how many bits arrive every second.
